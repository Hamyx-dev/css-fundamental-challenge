Bank of Ghana Maintains Policy Rate at 14% Amid Global Uncertainty

The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) held its 130th regular meeting from May 18 to 20, 2026, to review recent economic developments and assess risks to the outlook for inflation and growth.

The decision was unanimous — all six MPC members voted to keep the benchmark rate unchanged at 14%, amid growing uncertainty in the global economic environment.

On the domestic economy, the MPC observed improved economic activity in the first quarter of 2026. Headline inflation rose marginally to 3.4% in April 2026 from 3.2% in March 2026 — the first increase since December 2024 — driven mainly by non-food inflation. Ghana's gross international reserves increased to $14.4 billion as of May 18, 2026.

"In taking the policy decision, the Committee acknowledged that ongoing geopolitical tensions in the Middle East have weakened the global growth outlook, intensified inflationary concerns, and heightened policy uncertainty, with potential implications for the domestic economy through trade and financial channels," said Dr. Johnson Pandit Asiama, Governor of the Bank of Ghana.

— Dr. Johnson Pandit Asiama, Governor, Bank of Ghana

The Governor also announced that the dynamic Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) framework would be amended to a uniform 20% reserve requirement maintained entirely in domestic currency, effective June 4, 2026.

The next MPC meeting is scheduled for July 22, 2026.
